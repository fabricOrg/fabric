---
title: Email deliverability
description: Practices that keep transactional email authenticated, trusted, and out of spam.
---

Deliverability is the odds that an accepted message reaches the inbox rather than spam or a bounce.
Fabric accepts and sends your mail; whether it lands depends mostly on authentication, reputation,
and content — which you control.

## Authenticate first

Nothing else matters until SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass and align with your visible `from` domain. See
[Domains & DNS](/docs/email/domains-dns/). Unauthenticated mail is the single most common reason a
message is filtered or rejected outright.

## Send the right mail

- **Keep transactional and marketing streams separate.** Mixing a promotional blast with receipts on
  the same domain drags your transactional reputation down with it.
- **Always include a plain-text part.** Supplying both `text` and `html` improves scoring and gives
  clients a fallback.
- **Send to addresses that expect you.** Only mail recipients who gave consent; a spike of unknown
  or invalid recipients looks like spam to receivers.

## Watch the signals

- **Bounces** — a message that a receiver rejects. Retrieve the `EmailMessage` and branch on its
  `errorCode`; stop sending to a hard-bouncing address.
- **`errorCode`** — stable and machine-readable. Log it with your business reference, never the
  recipient content.
- **Volume ramp** — a brand-new domain has no reputation. Increase live volume gradually rather than
  sending your entire list on day one.

## Verify in sandbox

Sandbox uses a fake provider, so you can exercise the full send → retrieve → webhook loop and your
bounce handling with deterministic outcomes before any real inbox is involved. Wire up your
consumers there, then authenticate a domain and ramp live volume.

:::tip[Reputation is per-domain]
Deliverability follows the authenticated sending domain, not the API. Protect it: authenticate, send
wanted mail, and react to bounces quickly.
:::
